# Postpartum Core Rebuild Essentials: From Diaphragmatic Breath to Progressive Loading

**Target Audience:** New mothers, postpartum women, postpartum return-to-fitness  
**Program Track:** Beginner Start Line  
**Estimated Read Time:** 5 minutes  

---

## Why Crunches and Planks Aren't the Answer

After pregnancy and delivery, whether vaginal or via Cesarean section, your abdominal wall, pelvic floor, and spinal stabilizers undergo profound changes. Common issues include **diastasis recti (abdominal separation)**, pelvic floor laxity, and chronic lower back fatigue.

Jumping directly into traditional high-pressure core exercises like crunches, heavy planks, or burpees increases intra-abdominal pressure downward and outward, worsening core separation.

Rebuilding requires a **three-phase progression**:
1. Breathwork & Deep Transverse Abdominis (TVA) Activation
2. Joint Stability & Isometric Control
3. Functional Progressive Overload

---

## Phase 1: 360-Degree Diaphragmatic Breathing

The diaphragm and pelvic floor work like a piston. Re-establishing this rhythm is step zero.

### The Technique:
1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor. Place one hand on your belly and one hand on your ribcage.
2. Inhale deeply through your nose, expanding your ribs 360 degrees outward into the floor (not just puffing your belly upward).
3. Exhale slowly through pursed lips. As you exhale, imagine gently zipping up a tight pair of pants from your pelvic floor upward to your navel.
4. Perform 10 slow, intentional breaths twice daily.

## 3 Essential Rehabilitation Movements

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. Heel Slides with Exhale Engagement  ->  8 reps / leg             |
|  2. Glute Bridges with Pelvic Neutral    ->  10 reps (3s hold)        |
|  3. Bird-Dog Iso-Holds                   ->  6 reps / side (5s hold)  |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Move 1: Supine Heel Slides
- **Execution:** Lie on your back. Exhale and engage your deep core, slowly sliding one heel along the floor until your leg is extended, then slide back. Your lower back must not arch.

### Move 2: Glute Bridge with Core Brace
- **Execution:** Drive through your heels to raise your hips into a straight line from knees to shoulders. Squeeze your glutes at the top without hyperextending your lumbar spine.

### Move 3: Bird-Dog (Quadruped Reach)
- **Execution:** On hands and knees, reach your opposite arm and leg straight out. Focus on zero hip shifting or torso rotation.

---

## Golden Rules Before Returning to Running or Jumping

- Always obtain clearance from your OB-GYN or pelvic health physiotherapist (typically at 6–8 weeks post-delivery).
- Watch for warning signs: pelvic heaviness, doming/coning along the midline of your abdomen, or urine leakage. If present, regress immediately.
